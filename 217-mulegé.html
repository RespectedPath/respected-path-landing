Mulegé

January 12, 2024 · Blog Posts

The pueblo of Mulegé was was first established in 1705 by Jesuit priest Juan Manuel Basaldua. In 1754 missionary Francisco Escalante ordered the building of El Mission de Nuestra Señora de Santa Rosalia de Mulegé. The Mulegé river flows through the center of town, lined with palm trees it offers a unique view as they share the landscape with the Saguaro cactus on the surrounding mountains.

Today, the town serves as the tourist hub for the beaches of the Bahia Concepcion, but doesn’t offer much more than a few hotels, restaurants and grocery stores for the passing tourist. It’s also home to a large contingent of America and Canadian expat retirees. Other than the Mission, which still stands, and a 19th century jail, which serves as the towns scant museum, the main attractions of Mulegé are the white sand beaches a calm turquoise blue water further south.

There are about 10-12 beaches accessible to cars along the trans peninsular highway. Popular with campers and RV owners most beaches lack any amenities other than maybe an outhouse. Some beaches are more crowded than others but none so crowded that you will find yourself annoyed by your fellow beach goers. Locals drop by in the morning offering eggs and bread and other necessities. For about $10 a night you can pitch a tent or park your camper, cell phone service is nonexistent and gives you an ideal place to disconnect, even if temporarily. I didn’t have neither a camper or a tent so choose to spend my days just lounging on the shores. The water is still too cold to swim in January but plenty of people found Kayaking and Paddleboarding to be great ways to enjoy the quiet waters.

Two full days are more than enough for a backpacker and soon I was on my way back south to Loreto to catch a bus further south to LaPaz.
