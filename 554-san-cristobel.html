San Cristobel

March 10, 2024 · Uncategorized

Due to its mountainous terrain, what is currently the Mexican state of Chiapas, was hardly touched by Spanish explorers until much later in history.  The city of San Cristobel de las Casas sits at 7,200 feet above sea level and has been a hub of indigenous culture for hundred of years, almost untouched by European influence. The tiled rooftops have a style that is unique in Mexico, felling more South American than Southern Mexico.

The main tourist draw for San Cristobel is Sumidero Canyon. What some call the Mexican Grand Canyon. In the rainy season its sides are lined with waterfalls and moneys and crocodiles. This time of year the crocodiles lie on the banks and the moneys make brief appearance on the branches of the trees. The waterfalls are completely dry, giving a glimpse to the rock formations underneath. The park is protected as a National Park so they only way in is on a boat tour, only given in Spanish. Oh well, I can always use the practice.

San Cristobel is where I started to have doubts on my travel plans. When I started this journery nearly two months ago I had grand plans to tour all of Central America. Heading south through Mexico, into the Yucatan, exiting in Belize and then spending a few months zig zagging through the rest of the isthmus until reaching the end of the road in Panama and evaluating my travel priorities and desires from there. Looking at my options from San Cristobal, the Yucatan started looking unappealing. I had already seen so much of Mexico at this point. Cancun being the main draw for the area was not sounding appealing as I had already seen its west coast cousin, Los Cabos a few weeks earlier. Tourist shuttles were being advertised to Antigua, Guatamala  for reasonable prices. I have to decisions to make.
