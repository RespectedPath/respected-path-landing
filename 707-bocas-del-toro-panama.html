Bocas Del Toro, Panama

May 12, 2024 · Uncategorized

The last 2 days of diving got scrubbed because of the weather. Morning rain storms made the water choppy and cloudy. Signs that Central America’s rainy season is just beginning. I packed y bag and made my way to the bus stop in Los Coco’s and started the day of travel back to San Jose. You can’t catch a tourist shuttle from Los coco’s to San Jose so I’m back to riding the local public transport. It’s much cheaper this way, anyways.

I arrived in San Jose at about 6pm and settled into the hostel where I would stay 2 nights. I wouldn’t have stayed this long, but I had some things I needed to get done that I could only accomplish in a bigger city, but more on that another time. The 2 days flew by as I was busy and the next thing, I know I’m packed up again and waiting outside for my hostel for transport to my next destination. A place in the northwest of Panama called Bocas del Toro, or Bull’s Mouth in English.

Boca’s is an archipelago of islands in the northeast of Panama in the Caribbean Sea. Believe it or not, this will be my fist (only visit) to the Caribbean side of the Central American isthmus on this trek. Another backpacker’s hub and discount vacation for the locals Bocas is host to another popular diving area. I must admit that by this point of the trip I’m exhausted of Colonial Cities and pre-Colombian history so diving will become the main attraction for a while, even though is a drain on the budget.

6 hours from San Jose we cross the Panamanian border another hour to a dock where we will get onto a small boat or lancha for another 45 min ride to the main island of Colón. This is where I will spend the better part of the week. There are other islands to explore, but not really wanting to pack up and move every couple days, I just booked a hostel bed and found a dive shop and bought a 3 days of diving package.

And again, I have to just explain what I saw since I don’t have a dive housing my camera. The reefs around Boca’s are full of various tropical fish and other sea life like Rays and Sharks. Although not nearly as lively as the reefs in Los Coco’s in Costa Rica.  There are even quite a few shipwrecks to see while we make our ways around the underwater world of Boca’s. It’s so eerie to be swimming around and have a giant sunken ship come into view from the haze and murkiness of the water. We were still able to view various smaller Rays and more Reef sharks. At this point thought, it really doesn’t matter to me what we do or don’t see, just being able to be underwater and have an entirely different world to explore is pretty cool.

My time in Boca’s came sooner rather than later. I lucked out and had the hostel room to myself for my final 2 nights there. The quietness is welcomed, but also a little odd as you grow accustomed to the commotion of a hostel dorm.
